What gets counted in a poll, a campaign or a budget shapes the story we hear. This notebook starts with a question about polling.

Notebook

Where does a local issue go in a national poll?

Imagine a town debating a new data centre. At a public meeting, people spend the evening on electricity use, construction jobs and who pays for new infrastructure. A national poll the same week asks voters for their single biggest concern. Almost nobody answers “data centres.”

The two results can sit comfortably together. One asks the people who live near a particular decision what they think about it. The other asks a much wider group to choose between every issue competing for their attention.

Three details make a ranking easier to interpret. Who was asked? Did respondents name their own issue or pick from a list? And could they choose more than one? I’d want those answers before drawing a conclusion from the order of the bars.
